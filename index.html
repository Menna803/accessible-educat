What Our Research Revealed

Our research explored accessibility and inclusion for students with disabilities in higher education. It brought together studies about physical environments, digital access, academic experiences, and social participation.

14 Studies included in a systematic review discussed in our research
322 Articles examined in a bibliometric and content analysis
15 Students involved in an eye-tracking experiment mentioned in the research
60%+ Participants in cited field measurements reporting physical accessibility problems
These figures are presented as reported in the studies collected for our project. They refer to different research samples and methods, not one single survey. The 60%+ figure relates to the field measurements discussed in our research, including studies in Ethiopian and Sri Lankan universities.
Finding 01

Physical Accessibility Matters

Inaccessible entrances, stairs, elevators, and restrooms can prevent students from reaching and using university facilities independently.

Finding 02

Digital Barriers Exist

Websites, learning platforms, and digital materials may not work well with screen readers or other assistive technologies.

Finding 03

Support and Attitudes Matter

Limited academic support, lack of awareness, and negative attitudes can affect participation and the student experience.

Understanding the Barriers

Our research groups the challenges into four connected areas.

🏛️

Physical Barriers

Steps without ramps, inaccessible classrooms, elevators, pathways, and restrooms.

💻

Digital Barriers

Inaccessible websites, learning platforms, documents, videos, and online services.

📚

Academic Barriers

Limited learning accommodations, unsuitable materials, and insufficient individual support.

🤝

Social Barriers

Negative assumptions, lack of awareness, exclusion, and limited participation in campus activities.

Rights, Not Privileges

Accessibility is connected to dignity, equality, and human rights. It is not something that students should have to earn or beg for.

Everyone has the right to learn and participate.

Article 24 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities recognizes the right to education and calls for inclusive education systems at all levels, including higher education.

This means removing discrimination, providing reasonable accommodation, and making the support needed for effective learning available.
